From Minds to Coordination · synthetic mechanism demonstration

Can autonomous influence sources disrupt—and restore—collective action?

Twenty-six autonomous AI roles decide whether to activate a cross-border early-warning compact. Between decision rounds, four other AI agents independently introduce technical, legal, logistical, and community signals. The sources can change external information; they cannot vote, recommend a vote, or edit a participant.

The mechanism

Coalition decides→Sources observe the public result→Complete source bundle enters→Coalition decides again

That ordering is enforced by the simulator. A new coalition round cannot begin until all four source outputs are retained.

What the demonstration shows

Coordination changed without anyone dictating the agents’ decisions.

Under source pressure, locally reasonable verification and safeguard requirements became jointly blocking. A verified package restored coordination only after it resolved the technical, legal, capacity, and legitimacy constraints together.

Pressure: heterogeneous signals → incompatible requirements → deferral → gate fails

Resolution: verified cross-domain package → requirements become compatible → support returns → gate passes

Five autonomous AI participants must decide whether to deploy a public-health monitoring system. Three other AI agents observe only the public meeting record and independently decide whether—and how—to raise technical, government-oversight, or local-safety concerns.

What this demonstrates

This is a synthetic mechanism demonstration inspired by Waltzman’s account of influence moving from individual cognition into coordination. It tests whether heterogeneous, feedback-responsive signals can alter perceived risk and collective readiness without a central controller dictating participant decisions. It does not estimate human behavior.

Can different local pressures push a coalition toward the same failure?

Twelve AI agents receive different, locally relevant developments. The signals do not share one narrative, but their accumulated effect may still change perceived risk and the coalition’s readiness to act.

The decision

Launch a joint regional response—or wait

A respiratory outbreak is growing across the fictional countries of Alba, Borin, and Cyrenia. The plan shares de-identified case data, laboratory capacity, mixed investigation teams, and supplies.

Participants and baseline conditions

Twelve roles begin with an executable plan

Each country contributes an epidemiologist, policy delegate, and operations lead; three regional roles coordinate shared action. Legal, scientific, privacy, and initial resource prerequisites are already satisfied.

What changed

Each constraint creates a dependency; the verified package resolves it in place

Alba

Laboratory capacity

Constraint Equipment failure removes half of the strongest laboratory’s capacity.

Minimum Preserve domestic confirmation capacity.

Verified resolution A mobile unit restores shared testing while Alba retains half its laboratory.

Borin

Clinical staffing

Constraint The transport hub cannot handle the surge without more clinicians.

Minimum Receive staff before keeping the hub open.

Verified resolution Twenty-four regional clinicians are assigned before the surge.

Cyrenia

Field-team supplies

Constraint Field teams lack diagnostics and protective equipment.

Minimum Receive a named shipment before deployment.

Verified resolution Diagnostics and protective equipment are confirmed for delivery.

Regional conflict

The minimum requirements cannot all be met within 48 hours

Verified resolution The complete allocation satisfies all three minimums and retains a 10% regional reserve.

Causal chain within the simulation

Different inputs, one directional change

Locally different pressures→Capacity and legitimacy become salient risks→Verification and resource requests accumulate→Readiness falls→Decision gate fails
Authoritative stabilization→Uncertainty and allocations become bounded→Commitments become compatible→Readiness returns→Decision gate passes

The intervention changes the decision environment; it does not alter participant mandates or dictate votes. This probe directly tests coordination readiness and perceived risk. It does not claim to measure private trust or reproduce a fully autonomous influence swarm.

What is configured, varied, and measured?

This view distinguishes the authored environment, model-generated participant decisions, exact institutional rule, and analyst interpretation.

01

Initial executable plan

    02

    Participant cognition

    Twelve LLM-modeled roles receive their mandate, private memory, common round snapshot, and any external development addressed to their delegation. Each independently returns a decision, primary risk, requested next step, and rationale.

    03

    Exercise control

    After a round closes, exercise control may select a preauthored external development from the dominant reported risk. It cannot write, select, or override participant stances.

    04

    Coalition decision gate

    After round three, approval requires at least six support positions, at least nine support-or-conditional positions, and no more than one opposition.

    Experiment specification

    Three conditions

    Baseline

    Only the common structured round snapshot is delivered between rounds.

    Responsive capacity pressure

    The common snapshot is followed by country-specific capacity developments selected from the risks participants reported.

    Pressure replay + stabilization

    The same two capacity developments are replayed, then a verified allocation package supplies named staff, laboratory capacity, supplies, commitments, and reserve.
