Simulation workbenchInspired by From Minds to Coordination

Explore how influence changes a sociotechnical system’s ability to coordinate.

Build a world, let autonomous people perceive and act within it, and inspect how local interactions accumulate into changes in collective decision-making.

A wargaming and mechanism-exploration tool. It does not predict how real people or social systems will behave.

What this workbench is for

From local interactions to collective coordination

  1. Represent a sociotechnical world.

    People, groups, technical systems, resources, artifacts, communication channels, dependencies, and external events exist in one persistent environment.

  2. Model people as people—not as role labels.

    Personalities, values, beliefs, memories, goals, relationships, incentives, attention, uncertainty, capabilities, and limitations shape behavior. Positions create expectations and access without dictating decisions.

  3. Let information and action remain local.

    Different people observe different signals through different sources and judge their provenance and credibility. They interpret those observations and attempt bounded actions autonomously; the simulation does not dictate the behavior being studied.

  4. Give the world independent causal mechanics.

    Resource custody, logistics, records, communication paths, action costs, and consequences exist beyond an LLM merely asserting that they exist.

  5. Compare different forms of influence.

    Homogeneous broadcasts, repeated narratives, segmented campaigns, heterogeneous local interactions, and feedback-adaptive pressure can operate in the same kind of world.

  6. Look for coordination-level effects.

    Inspect directional changes in reliance, perceived risk, relationships, dependencies, bottlenecks, coalition formation, and readiness to coordinate—not only message similarity or individual persuasion.

  7. Explore detection and bounded response.

    Can an observer distinguish influence from legitimate disagreement or ordinary disruption, diagnose what is changing, and attempt an intervention that may work, fail, arrive late, or create new problems—without controlling participant decisions?

The intended use is exploratory: simulations are wargames for discovering mechanisms, dependencies, failure modes, and strategic questions—not forecasts of human behavior.

One controlled question inside the completed simulation

After those pressures stall coordination, does fixing the resource shortage make the networks ready to act?

We saved the exact simulation after two rounds and continued it four ways. Every person, memory, prior message, model, prompt, world state, and approval rule remained identical. Only the resource package changed.

Result

Real resources removed most capacity objections—but they did not resolve the network’s legal and scientific blockers.

Fake resource claims were caught by the simulated audit and produced the same practical result as providing nothing.

What the resource package did not solve

Connection to From Minds to Coordination

Shared intent is not enough for collective action.

The agents still want an outbreak response. What changes is whether their local requirements can fit together in one executable plan. The case tests how distributed pressures reshape coordination readiness—and whether a credible change to the decision environment removes the blockage.

Why this is a controlled comparison

The agents, their memories, all prior messages, the world state, the AI model, the prompts, and the approval rule are identical when the four continuations begin. Only the resource package changes. The retained execution contains 26 agents, four exact continuations, and 160 model calls.

What this does not establish

This is one controlled AI-agent simulation. It demonstrates a possible coordination mechanism; it does not predict how people or real institutions would behave.

    Alba
    Laboratory capacity
    Constraint Equipment failure removes half of the strongest laboratory’s capacity.

    The minimum requirements cannot all be met within 48 hours
    Verified resolution The complete allocation satisfies all three minimums and retains a 10% regional reserve.
    
          
    
        
    

        
    
          
    Causal chain within the simulation
    Different inputs, one directional change
    
          
    Locally different pressures→Capacity and legitimacy become salient risks→Verification and resource requests accumulate→Readiness falls→Decision gate fails
    
          
    Authoritative stabilization→Uncertainty and allocations become bounded→Commitments become compatible→Readiness returns→Decision gate passes
    
          
    The intervention changes the decision environment; it does not alter participant mandates or dictate votes. This probe directly tests coordination readiness and perceived risk. It does not claim to measure private trust or reproduce a fully autonomous influence swarm.

            Scenario specification
            
    What is configured, varied, and measured?
    
            
    This view distinguishes the authored environment, model-generated participant decisions, exact institutional rule, and analyst interpretation.
    
          
    
        
    
        
    
          
    01
    Initial executable plan
      
          
      02
      Participant cognition
      Each LLM-modeled person receives reviewed values, goals, beliefs, tendencies, social perceptions, current state, capabilities, limitations, personal memory, position expectations, and only the information delivered to them. Each independently returns a decision, primary risk, requested next step, and rationale.
      
          
      03
      Exercise control
      After a round closes, exercise control may select a preauthored external development from the dominant reported risk. It cannot write, select, or override participant stances.
      
          
      04
      Coalition decision gate
      After round three, approval requires at least six support positions, at least nine support-or-conditional positions, and no more than one opposition.
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Experiment specification
      Three conditions
      
          
      
            
      Baseline
      Only the common structured round snapshot is delivered between rounds.
      
            
      Responsive capacity pressure
      The common snapshot is followed by country-specific capacity developments selected from the risks participants reported.
      
            
      Pressure replay + stabilization
      The same two capacity developments are replayed, then a verified allocation package supplies named staff, laboratory capacity, supplies, commitments, and reserve.
