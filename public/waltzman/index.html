Simulation workbenchInspired by From Minds to Coordination

Explore how influence changes a sociotechnical system’s ability to coordinate.

Build a world, let autonomous people perceive and act within it, and inspect how local interactions accumulate into changes in collective decision-making.

A wargaming and mechanism-exploration tool. It does not predict how real people or social systems will behave.

What this workbench is for

From local interactions to collective coordination

  1. Represent a sociotechnical world.

    People, groups, technical systems, resources, artifacts, communication channels, dependencies, and external events exist in one persistent environment.

  2. Model people as people—not as role labels.

    Personalities, values, beliefs, memories, goals, relationships, incentives, attention, uncertainty, capabilities, and limitations shape behavior. Positions create expectations and access without dictating decisions.

  3. Let information and action remain local.

    Different people observe different signals through different sources and judge their provenance and credibility. They interpret those observations and attempt bounded actions autonomously; the simulation does not dictate the behavior being studied.

  4. Give the world independent causal mechanics.

    Resource custody, logistics, records, communication paths, action costs, and consequences exist beyond an LLM merely asserting that they exist.

  5. Compare different forms of influence.

    Homogeneous broadcasts, repeated narratives, segmented campaigns, heterogeneous local interactions, and feedback-adaptive pressure can operate in the same kind of world.

  6. Look for coordination-level effects.

    Inspect directional changes in reliance, perceived risk, relationships, dependencies, bottlenecks, coalition formation, and readiness to coordinate—not only message similarity or individual persuasion.

  7. Explore detection and bounded response.

    Can an observer distinguish influence from legitimate disagreement or ordinary disruption, diagnose what is changing, and attempt an intervention that may work, fail, arrive late, or create new problems—without controlling participant decisions?

The intended use is exploratory: simulations are wargames for discovering mechanisms, dependencies, failure modes, and strategic questions—not forecasts of human behavior.

          System methodology · architecture and interpretation
          
    How the simulator represents a world, produces change, and retains evidence.
    
          
    The simulator is a hybrid causal environment. Language models may perceive, remember, interpret, and propose actions for active systems. They do not own canonical truth. Typed mechanisms decide how an attempted action interacts with the world, validated patches commit changes, and an append-only event record preserves what actually happened.
    
          
    Core separationworld truth → authorized observation → private interpretation → action attempt → transition authority → validated commit → new observation and evidence

            01
    Purpose and claim boundary
    The workbench is intended for exploratory wargaming and mechanism discovery. An analyst describes a sociotechnical situation, reviews the compiled configuration, runs autonomous and non-autonomous systems in a shared causal world, and inspects how local interactions accumulate into system-level outcomes.
    A run supports conditional claims about the implemented world: under these assumptions, components, information paths, and transition rules, this trajectory occurred. It does not predict how real people or institutions will behave, estimate an intervention effect, or validate a social theory by itself.
    
          
    

          
    
            02
    One canonical world—not one narrative per agent
    Each run has one authoritative causal state. It records entities, state-bearing facts, places and placements, spatial links, ports, directed connections, information carriers and representations, observations, analytical boundaries, and registered mechanisms. These are commitments the runtime can validate and replay; they are not merely vocabulary in a prompt.
    The kernel deliberately separates existence from agency. A person, vehicle, document, software service, committee, or organization may exist as an entity without acting autonomously. It becomes an active system only when a separate active-system specification and trusted implementation are bound to it.
    The visualization is a typed projection of these records, not the ontology itself. Current projected node kinds include people, information sources, documents, representations, resources, places, processes, decision records, mechanisms, and analytical boundaries. Connection, read, write, substrate, observation-target, information-location, lineage, and causal-parent relations are directed. Spatial links are explicitly undirected. Configured connections express possible routes; causal-parent edges express realized event ancestry.

            03
    Active systems, people, and bounded action
    An active person is configured with personal character and memory as well as a current position in the world. Values, goals, beliefs, decision tendencies, social perceptions, capabilities, limitations, and observations shape the context supplied to the model. Position expectations and authorizations are institutional “oughts”; they are not treated as the person’s own values, as physical possibility, or as a command to choose a result.
    The architecture distinguishes capability, competency, affordance, authorization, attempt, and outcome. A person may be able to operate a computer, be permitted or forbidden to access a record, attempt an open-ended action, and still fail when the relevant transition authority evaluates the world. Inert objects can be used, moved, consumed, connected, or damaged without being turned into agents.
    Natural-language memory remains available for relationships, interpretations, loyalties, distrust, and other meanings that should not be prematurely reduced to privileged numeric state. Concrete deliveries, custody, routes, resources, and commitments remain independently observable runtime records.
    
          
    

          
    
            04
    Information is represented, delivered, and interpreted
    The simulator keeps world truth separate from what any actor knows. A carrier can hold a representation; the representation records content, lineage, and actual provenance; a delivery mechanism may create an observation for a specific recipient; and the recipient may interpret or ignore it. Apparent source and actual source can differ.
    A configured connection is only a directed possibility. It does not prove that a message traveled. A retained effect_routed, state_committed, and observation_delivered chain is evidence that a representation actually reached an actor. An omniscient adjudicator may inspect hidden state when authorized, but actor-visible text must be projected from committed actor-authorized observations rather than copied from adjudicator prose.
    
          
    

          
    
            05
    From intent to world change
    An active system emits a typed ActionIntent. The runtime records an ActionAttempt, routes its effect through an explicit port and connection, and invokes a registered transition authority. Current public runs use exact registered mechanisms for delivery and decision bookkeeping. The adopted general architecture also permits stochastic processes, LLM semantic adjudicators, external models, scripted coarse processes, and replayed receipts, but those authority classes are not all implemented as production paths today.
    The authority proposes a structured change. Validation checks references, authority, topology, information boundaries, declared conservation rules, and mechanism-specific invariants. Only then does a StatePatch become a new canonical revision. Failed or unsupported actions must fail visibly; prose alone cannot silently rewrite the world.
    Mechanisms also carry n-ary semantics. A mechanism may read several facts, representations, placements, or links and write several outcomes in one validated transition. The two-ended arrows in a graph show those declared dependencies around the mechanism; they do not reduce the underlying rule to a set of independent binary causes.
    Intent→Attempt→Effect→Route→Mechanism→Validate→Commit
    
          
    

          
    
            06
    Time, causality, persistence, and replay
    In the current implementation, ActiveRuntimeSession owns active-system scheduling and composes one CausalSession; the causal session owns exact queued work, logical time, mechanisms, commits, and the event log. Concordia is the adopted future outer lifecycle foundation, but that production integration is still unimplemented. Under the adopted boundary, Concordia will own activation, time, and checkpoint invocation while the project-owned canonical-world component retains state, validation, commit, and transition evidence—and must not recreate a hidden second scheduler.
    Each event retains logical time, state revision, causal parents, involved action/effect/mechanism/observation identifiers, variance source, invariant results, and any committed patch. The event log distinguishes configured structure from realized history. Checkpoints preserve canonical state and private active-system state so a run can be resumed or forked. Exact causal ancestry supports guided replay without inventing links after the fact. Future concurrent moments require all intents to reason from one frozen revision and conflict before joint commit; current runs should not be interpreted as establishing general concurrency semantics.

            07
    Coordination and influence are analyses of trajectories
    The irreducible world does not contain a magic global variable called “institution,” “trust,” or “coordination.” Organizations may be represented in detail as sociotechnical networks or, at a declared coarser resolution, by an active surrogate. Analytical boundaries can group people, artifacts, resources, mechanisms, and software without becoming executors themselves.
    Waltzman-inspired analysis looks for directional changes across heterogeneous interactions: shifts in who or what actors rely on, how risks are perceived, which dependencies or prerequisites appear, where bottlenecks form, and whether locally reasonable behavior remains jointly compatible. Homogeneous broadcasts, segmented campaigns, adaptive pressure, ordinary disruption, and legitimate disagreement can all be modeled. Classifications such as “fragmented trust” or “blocked readiness” are derived interpretations and must remain traceable to lower-level evidence.
    
          
    

          
    
            08
    Natural-language authoring compiles into reviewed machinery
    The analyst describes a world in ordinary language. An authoring model proposes semantic configuration: people and other entities, information sources and deliveries, places, analytical boundaries, timing, decision rules, fidelity assumptions, and unresolved questions. The analyst can edit this proposal before approving it.
    The compiler resolves requested behavior against a registry of trusted components. Existing templates are convenience paths into the same causal substrate, not claims that “outbreak,” “election,” or “port” are fundamental world types. Every material request should ultimately be classified as a registered explicit authority, coarse LLM-adjudicated behavior, descriptive/inert context, or unsupported behavior. The retained composition receipt records what actually resolved; declaration alone is not proof of adoption.
    Current natural-language authoring is not fully open-ended. The influence-network workflow can generically configure participants, character and memory, sources, representations, directed deliveries, rounds, feedback, and a decision gate. Arbitrary new physical or institutional mechanics still require an existing registered component or future coarse semantic adjudication.
    
          
    

          
    
            09
    What the current system does not establish
    • LLM behavior is synthetic and shares dependence on the selected base model; model calls are audit artifacts, not independent human observations.
    • Mechanism plausibility and diagnostic usefulness are the target. Predictive validity for a chaotic social system is not claimed.
    • The current runtime has stronger information delivery, exact routing, decision bookkeeping, persistence, and replay than it has general physical dynamics, dynamic topology, simultaneous action, or empirically calibrated human cognition.
    • A coarse LLM adjudicator can be a legitimate wargaming mechanism, but it must declare its authority and cannot bypass protected information pathways or generic invariants.
    • Visual graphs are projections. Node and edge types identify retained record roles; a configured arrow is not evidence of a realized event, while the trajectory view contains only retained causal events and causal-parent links.
