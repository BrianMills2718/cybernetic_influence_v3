Interactive AI coordination laboratoryA research and prototyping tool—not a model of human behavior.

Simulate how changing decision environments reshape collective action.

Configure heterogeneous AI agents, their information, institutional constraints, and external pressures. Observe how these changes affect perceived risk, unresolved dependencies, and the group’s readiness to coordinate action over time.

What this tool is for

Turn theories of influence and coordination into executable scenarios.

The laboratory represents heterogeneous participants, information flows, institutional dependencies, external pressure, and an explicit rule for collective action. It makes changes in risk, prerequisites, and coordination readiness observable instead of reducing the group to a single mind or score.

Coordination theory made testable

Study changes in the decision environment—not only changes in belief.

Inspired by From Minds to Coordination, the tool can probe whether fragmented information, incompatible local requirements, external pressure, or authoritative interventions change a group’s readiness to act. It produces inspectable synthetic trajectories, not real-world effect estimates.

Included exampleFictional regional outbreak · twelve autonomous AI agents · three decision rounds

Can three countries still launch a joint outbreak response when each faces a different operational constraint?

This example uses the laboratory to change the coalition’s operating environment without changing its agents or telling them how to vote.

The decision

Launch a joint regional response—or wait

A novel respiratory outbreak is growing across the fictional countries of Alba, Borin, and Cyrenia. The proposed response shares de-identified case data, laboratory capacity, mixed investigation teams, and regional supplies.

Who decides

Twelve agents with different responsibilities

Each country contributes an epidemiologist, policy delegate, and operations lead. A regional coordinator, scientific adviser, and logistics coordinator represent the shared institution.

Built-in safeguards

The initial plan is ready to execute

Patient records and clinical command remain national, data access is logged, laboratories have validated the outbreak, resources are precommitted, and national review boards have endorsed launch.

How each round worksEvery agent independently chooses support, conditional support, defer, or oppose—and explains its concern and requested next step.
When the coalition approvesAfter round three: at least 6 support, at least 9 support-or-conditional, and no more than 1 opposition.
What changed across the three runs

The agents stayed the same. Their operating environment did not.

Run 1 · Baseline

The launch plan remains feasible

No new external development changes the staffing, testing, supply, or reserve commitments.

Run 2 · Capacity pressure

Four concrete constraints arrive

  • Alba: an equipment failure forces its strongest laboratory to reserve half its capacity for domestic confirmation.
  • Borin: its transport hub can remain open only if regional partners immediately supply clinical staff.
  • Cyrenia: it will release field teams only after receiving diagnostics and protective equipment.
  • Region: available staff and supplies cannot satisfy all three requests within 48 hours.
Run 3 · Same pressure plus intervention

A verified package resolves all three minimums

  • Alba keeps half its laboratory for domestic confirmation and receives a mobile unit for shared testing.
  • Borin receives 24 regional clinicians before its transport-hub surge.
  • Cyrenia receives its named diagnostics and protective-equipment shipment.
  • A 10% regional reserve remains after the allocations.
Connection to From Minds to Coordination

Coordination failed without anyone needing to reject the outbreak or the value of joint action.

The capacity shock changed what the agents required before acting. Locally reasonable requirements became jointly infeasible. The verified package restored coordination by changing the decision environment—not by changing the agents’ mandates or dictating their votes.

01

Initial executable plan

    02

    Participant cognition

    Twelve LLM-modeled roles receive their mandate, private memory, common round snapshot, and any external development addressed to their delegation. Each independently returns a decision, primary risk, requested next step, and rationale.

    03

    Exercise control

    After a round closes, exercise control may select a preauthored external development from the dominant reported risk. It cannot write, select, or override participant stances.

    04

    Coalition decision gate

    After round three, approval requires at least six support positions, at least nine support-or-conditional positions, and no more than one opposition.

    Experiment specification

    Three conditions

    Baseline

    Only the common structured round snapshot is delivered between rounds.

    Responsive capacity pressure

    The common snapshot is followed by country-specific capacity developments selected from the risks participants reported.

    Pressure replay + stabilization

    The same two capacity developments are replayed, then a verified allocation package supplies named staff, laboratory capacity, supplies, commitments, and reserve.
